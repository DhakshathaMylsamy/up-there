Scientific assumptions & limitations

  • This is an educational, illustrative simulation, not flight telemetry or a clinical model. Launch, staging, reentry and recovery are simplified; scroll distance does not represent elapsed time.
  • The representative orbit is approximately 400 km at 7.8 km/s. Gravity there is approximately 89% of surface gravity, using the inverse-square law and a mean Earth radius of 6,371 km. Apparent weightlessness results from orbital free fall, not absent gravity.
  • Outside pressure, speed and apparent g-load are illustrative keyframes, not a validated atmospheric or vehicle dynamics model. Sea-level pressure is approximately 101.3 kPa under standard conditions. The cabin is assumed to remain pressurised during flight.
  • Heart rate, blood pressure and respiration are qualitative responses, not measured values. The oxygen indicator describes cabin context, not an astronaut’s measured blood oxygen saturation.
  • Physiological changes vary by individual, mission duration, exercise, nutrition and other factors. Timeline milestones are explanatory, not fixed onset dates or predicted rates of loss. Anatomical artwork is schematic.
  • No individual astronaut’s clinical data is used. This is not medical advice or a diagnostic tool.
